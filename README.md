## Project Domain
### Mission Statement
The mission of the database is to organize and abstract magic spells for Dungeons and Dragons players.
### Objectives
- Organize each spell into its respective class
- Abstract spells so that they are more easily accessed for both players and DMs
- Store what each spell is capable of when cast. 
- Manage spells so that they can be accessed or compared with others without much focus
- List the requirements to cast each spell
### Subjects
- Classes
- Spells
- Levels
- Duration
- Casting Time
- Range
- School of Magic
- Book source
### Description
The list of Dungeons and Dragons spells is massive, 450-550 individual spells. Far too big to be memorized. The physical books that I’ve bought organize them in alphabetical order, which is almost completely useless, as there are at least 5 different specifications that would make more sense to organize them by (level, class, school of magic, heck, offensive vs defensive magic would make more sense!). Wizards of the Coast (the company that owns D&D) has a digital search engine on their app, but you need to purchase the digital version of the book that holds the specific spell you are searching for if you want to look at its properties. Now, of course you could look online and see if some guy on reddit has posted the spell, but a stray comment in an online argument is hardly reliable. I want to make a reliable and accessible database to list out the spells I have already paid for without needing to spend more money trying to access them digitally. All spells have very similar properties (level, duration, materials) that would stay consistent across the board, with different classes having access to a select, but often overlapping, list of spells. For this reason, I think that making the database relational would make the most sense.
